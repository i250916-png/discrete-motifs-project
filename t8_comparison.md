# T8 - How do the two departments communicate differently? (R = 3259)

Department A (ID 4, 109 people, 1,167 internal edges) has a strong, statistically significant communication structure, while Department B (ID 16, 25 people, 236 internal edges) is statistically indistinguishable from a random network with the same degrees. In Department A only two classes are motifs: Class 8 (Z = +25.85; observed 3,498 vs. about 917 expected) and Class 13 (Z = +28.58; observed 967 vs. about 191 expected). Both consist entirely of reciprocated (two-way) edges: Class 8 is a person who exchanges emails in both directions with two others who do not email each other, i.e. a two-way hub-and-spoke pattern, and Class 13 is a fully mutual triangle of three people who all write to each other. Nine classes (1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10, 11) are anti-motifs with Z between -5.67 and -18.06; these are the triads built mostly from one-way edges (out-stars, in-stars, chains, the transitive and cyclic triads), which are far rarer than chance. Class 3 (Z = +0.19) and Class 12 (Z = +0.26) are at the random level. So Department A communicates through reciprocal conversations that cluster around hubs and into tightly knit mutual groups, rather than through one-way broadcasts or chains of command. Department B has no motif and no anti-motif: all 13 Z-scores lie between -1.54 and +1.97 (Class 3 is the closest to the threshold at +1.97, just under 2), so its triad pattern can be explained by its degree sequence alone and there is no evidence of any special structure. A caution: Department B is small (25 nodes) and some of its random counts vary a lot between networks (for example Class 8 has a standard deviation of about 14), so a test with only 24 random networks has less power to detect a motif there; the correct conclusion is that no significant motif was found in Department B, not that it is proven to have no structure.
